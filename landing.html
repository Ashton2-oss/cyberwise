Golden rules

If you remember nothing else, remember these five.

The habits that stop most everyday attacks. Short enough to memorise — worth reading twice.

  1. 01
    Pause before you click.
    Urgency is the attacker's favourite tool. Ten seconds of hesitation stops most of them.
  2. 02
    Verify through another channel.
    Never trust a phone number, link, or address that came inside the message itself.
  3. 03
    Never share passwords or OTPs.
    No legitimate bank, IT team, or company will ever ask for them — in any channel.
  4. 04
    Unique passwords, plus two-factor.
    Attackers don't break in — they log in with credentials leaked from somewhere else. Don't reuse.
  5. 05
    Report early. Speed limits damage.
    There is no shame in reporting — every hour without it gives the attacker more room to move.
That's the five-second check. Everything else is detail.

About CyberWise

Cyber safety, grounded in student life.

CyberWise is a free learning tool that helps students recognise phishing, protect their accounts, and know what to do after a suspicious click. It brings together plain-language guidance, interactive practice, and practical recovery steps.

Why it exists

Built by a student, for students.

Created by Ashton Nyangani, a second-year Electrical Engineering student at the University of Johannesburg, CyberWise focuses on scams and digital situations South African students may actually encounter.

Examples include:

  • Fake bank alerts
  • University messages
  • Courier scams
  • Phishing links
  • WhatsApp forwards
The approach

Useful, clear, and local.

Learn at your own pace with realistic examples, short explanations, and next steps you can use in everyday life — without needing a technical background.

Progress is kept in your browser on this device. You can practise without creating an account or sharing a score.

Private by design.

Your learning progress stays on your device. CyberWise has no accounts, tracking, or ads.
